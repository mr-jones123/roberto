# Roberto Speaker Script (2–3 minutes)

## Opening (before Slide 1)
Good day everyone. We built **Roberto** to answer one urgent climate question: how do we turn flood risk data into decisions that protect people in real time?

Key framing: Roberto is **user-centered at the front door** and **rescuer-centered in operations**. Citizens are the signal; rescuers are the response engine — Roberto connects both in one real-time workflow.

## Self Intro (new intro slide)
Hi, I'm **Xy**. I am a full-stack developer at **HeyApril**, a US startup building a tax SaaS optimizer for creators. I previously worked as an AI Engineer Intern at **AIFirst** and as an AI QA Engineer at **Boost Capital**, a Singapore startup.

---

## Slide 1 — The Problem
Every typhoon, families face rising water before help can arrive. In the Philippines, flood exposure is constant, so speed and local context matter more than generic alerts.

## Slide 2 — The Gap
Today, coordination is fragmented: city-wide warnings are broad, field reports are manual, and people nearby who can help are often disconnected. The result is delayed action during the most critical minutes.

## Slide 3 — Introducing Roberto
Roberto is a disaster response network for Metro Manila. It connects community reporting, operational coordination, and risk intelligence in one shared map experience.

## Slide 4 — Coverage Intelligence
Before a crisis, Roberto shows where hazard is high and protection is weak by combining NOAH hazard zones, DPWH flood-control projects, and city boundaries. This gives LGUs and communities a clearer picture of where exposure remains.

## Slide 5 — Incident Command
During an incident, reports move through a clear human workflow: **PING to VERIFIED to PRIORITIZED to ASSIGNED to RESOLVED**. Citizens report, coordinators decide, responders execute.

## Slide 6 — Community Network
Beyond command-and-control, Roberto enables nearby people to connect and help each other. This turns bayanihan into a practical, location-aware support network.

## Slide 7 — AI Guidance
For each ping, Roberto adds context-aware guidance using hazard level, weather, and location. When AI confidence is low, it safely falls back to conservative preparedness steps.

## Slide 8 — Coordinator View
Coordinators get KPI visibility, incident filtering, and conflict-safe status updates with audit trails. This keeps accountability with people, not with a black-box model.

## Slide 9 — Responder View
Responders receive exact coordinates, incident context, and nearby critical facilities to act faster in the field. Resolution notes close the loop and preserve what happened.

## Slide 10 — Accessibility
The system is bilingual in English and Filipino so warnings and actions are understandable under stress. If people cannot understand guidance quickly, the system fails.

## Slide 11 — Architecture
Roberto is built on open data and practical infrastructure: React + Mapbox on the frontend, Express + SQLite on the backend, SSE for realtime updates, and role-based access controls.

## Slide 12 — Closing Answer
So how can technology help humanity live in right relationship with Earth's natural systems?

By starting with nature's signals first — where water flows, where risk concentrates, and where protection is incomplete — then translating that into local, human-led action.

Roberto does not try to "beat" the flood. It helps communities **understand, adapt, and coordinate** so fewer people face climate risk alone.
